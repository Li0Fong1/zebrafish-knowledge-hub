Q: What are the functions of the zebrafish nanog gene? Answer briefly.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
**Zebrafish nanog (UniProt · Bgee · Expression Atlas) encodes a maternal/zygotic homeodomain transcription factor that maintains pluripotency and coordinates early embryonic genome activation, cell viability, and patterning.**

- Maintains stem cell population and regulates DNA-templated transcription via RNA Pol II cis-regulatory regions (ENSDARG00000075113 (Ensembl); ZFIN ZDB-GENE-030131-5486 (ZFIN)).
- Maternal Nanog promotes oogenesis and is required for embryo architecture and cell viability during gastrulation.
- With Pou5f3/SoxB1/Sox19b, Nanog remodels chromatin and primes genes for zygotic genome activation.
- Nanog restricts maternal β-catenin/TCF activity, preventing excessive mesendoderm induction; Pou5f3–Nanog complexes pattern dorsoventral mesendoderm.
- It limits primordial germ cell number by suppressing vasa via nlk1, and has a primary role in extra-embryonic tissue.

Q: What factors determine the external morphology (body shape and pigmentation) of zebrafish? Answer briefly.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
External morphology (body shape and pigmentation) in zebrafish is determined by genetic/developmental programs, endocrine regulation—especially thyroid hormone—and environmental/chemical exposures.

- Genetic factors establish the baseline body plan, fin/body proportions, and pigment-cell development.
- Thyroid endocrine status influences body shape; thyroid disruption is associated with altered external morphology [1].
- Environmental/chemical exposures can modify morphology, such as glyphosate/Roundup altering body shape and behavior [3].
- Some external compounds may have little effect: N-2-fluorophenyl-D-gluconamide did not affect zebrafish embryogenesis in one study [2].

References:
[1] Thyroid endocrine disruption and external body morphology of Zebrafish. General and comparative endocrinology. 2016. source
[2] External N-2-fluorophenyl-D-gluconamide does not affect zebrafish embryogenesis. Cryobiology. 2026. source
[3] Glyphosate and Roundup(®) alter morphology and behavior in zebrafish. Toxicology. 2017. source

Q: What does the literature report on CRISPR-based studies of zebrafish morphogenesis? Provide inline citations.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
CRISPR-based studies in zebrafish have been used to phenocopy morphogenesis defects in F0 embryos and to screen for genes required for organ morphogenesis. Predesigned in vitro transcribed sgRNAs targeting Rx3 in F0 embryos partially recapitulated Rx3-dependent eye morphogenesis defects [1]. A rapid CRISPR/Cas mutagenesis assay was also applied to identify genes involved in thyroid morphogenesis and function [2]. These reports indicate that transient F0 CRISPR knockout is feasible for candidate validation and screening in zebrafish morphogenesis.

References:
[1] CRISPR-Cas9 F0 knockout approach using predesigned in vitro transcribed guide RNAs partially recapitulates Rx3 function in eye morphogenesis. Journal of genetics. 2023. source

[10/39]  Q01    （2.4 · T1-1）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: What determines the external morphology of zebrafish?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Zebrafish external morphology is determined by genotype, environment, and their interaction (phenotype = genotype + environment + G×E).

- Genotype: developmental gene regulatory networks specify body axis, fin position/shape, craniofacial structures, and pigmentation; genetic variation produces heritable morphological differences.
- Endocrine environment: thyroid hormone status influences body morphology; thyroid endocrine disruption alters zebrafish external body morphology [1].
- Chemical environment: exposure to glyphosate or Roundup® changes morphology [3]; effects are compound-specific, since N-2-fluorophenyl-D-gluconamide did not affect embryogenesis [2].
- G×E: the same genetic background can show different morphology depending on exposure, and different genotypes can differ in susceptibility.

References:
[1] Thyroid endocrine disruption and external body morphology of Zebrafish. General and comparative endocrinology. 2016. source
[2] External N-2-fluorophenyl-D-gluconamide does not affect zebrafish embryogenesis. Cryobiology. 2026. source
[3] Glyphosate and Roundup(®) alter morphology and behavior in zebrafish. Toxicology. 2017. source

[11/39]  Q02    （2.4 · T1-2）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Which factors determine body colour in zebrafish?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Zebrafish body colour is determined mainly by chromatophore type, number and spatial patterning under genetic control, with environmental light/background and temperature as modulating factors.

- Genetic/developmental factors: melanophores, iridophores and xanthophores interact to form stripes; iridophore proliferation and aggregation prefigure striped colouration [4].
- Background adaptation: background colour alters melanophore responses, and cyclical training enhances these physiological colour changes [2].
- Physical environment: LED light colour and increasing temperature co-exposure can alter pigmentation-related molecular, metabolomic and behavioural endpoints in larvae [1].
- Natural/sexual selection: colouration pattern variation is associated with reproductive success, which can shape colour traits in populations [3].

References:
[1] Co-exposure effect of different colour of LED lights and increasing temperature on zebrafish larvae (Danio rerio): Immunohistochemical, metabolomics, molecular and behaviour approaches. The Science of the total environment. 2024. source
[2] Cyclical training enhances the melanophore responses of zebrafish to background colours. Pigment cell & melanoma research. 2008. source
[3] 'Love at first sight': The effect of personality and colouration patterns in the reproductive success of zebrafish (Danio rerio). PloS one. 2018. source
[4] Proliferation, dispersal and patterned aggregation of iridophores in the skin prefigure striped colouration of zebrafish. Nature cell biology. 2014. source

[12/39]  Q04    （2.4 · T1-4）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Is body size in zebrafish related to stocking density?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes—body size in zebrafish is related to stocking density.
Higher stocking density generally reduces growth rate and final body size through increased competition for food, crowding stress, and poorer water quality.
Lower density usually allows greater food intake per fish and less stress, so fish tend to grow larger.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: What is the function of shha?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
shha (UniProt (Q92008) · Bgee · Expression Atlas) encodes zebrafish Sonic hedgehog a (Shha), a secreted Hedgehog morphogen that patterns the neural tube, brain, retina, pharyngeal arches, and fin/scale structures.

- Gene/ID: shha (ENSDARG00000068567 (Ensembl); ZFIN: ZDB-GENE-980526-166 (ZFIN)), chromosome 7.
- Neural tube: Shha from notochord/floor plate induces floor plate and motor neuron formation.
- Forebrain: shha-expressing cells mark the teleost pallidal signaling center and contribute to preglomerular complex and posterior tubercular dopamine cells.
- Retina: Shh promotes cell-cycle exit by activating p57Kip2.
- Pharyngeal arches: Bmp and Shh signaling regulate satb2 expression.
- Fin/limb and scales: Shh functions in fin/limb patterning networks involving 5'Hox and Gli3, and is expressed during scale development.

[14/39]  Q08    （2.4 · T1-8）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: How is hif1aa related to hypoxia?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
hif1aa (UniProt · Bgee · Expression Atlas) encodes zebrafish hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha a (ENSDARG00000006181 (Ensembl); ZDB-GENE-080917-55 (ZFIN)), the oxygen-sensitive subunit of HIF-1, and is a central mediator of the hypoxia response.

- Under normoxia, hif1aa protein is hydroxylated and targeted for degradation, keeping HIF-1 activity low.
- Under hypoxia, degradation is inhibited, allowing hif1aa to stabilize, enter the nucleus, and dimerize with the HIF-1β partner.
- The resulting complex binds hypoxia-response elements and activates genes for angiogenesis, erythropoiesis, and metabolic adaptation; in zebrafish, hif1a/VEGF-A signaling promotes hypoxia-related angiogenesis.
- Its GO annotation includes DNA binding and regulation of DNA-templated transcription, consistent with an oxygen-regulated transcription factor.

Q: What are the common morphological differences between the AB and TU strains?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
In zebrafish, AB and TU are both wild-type laboratory strains and look broadly similar. The usual morphological differences are subtle:

- AB adults tend to be slightly larger / stockier, with more regular lateral stripes.

These are not absolute differences and can be affected by rearing conditions and subline background, so they are not reliable for strain identification by appearance alone.

[33/39]  Q24    （2.5 · T7-24）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Compare the relationship between tp53 and mdm2 in zebrafish.
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
In zebrafish, tp53 (UniProt (P79734) · Bgee · Expression Atlas) and mdm2 (ZFIN (ZDB-GENE-990415-153) · Ensembl (ENSDARG00000033443) · UniProt · Bgee · Expression Atlas) form a conserved negative-regulatory axis: Mdm2 is the main p53 suppressor, and loss or dysregulation of mdm2 releases p53-dependent growth suppression and apoptosis.

- Zebrafish tp53 (ENSDARG00000035559 (Ensembl); ZFIN ZDB-GENE-990415-270 (ZFIN)) is the tumor suppressor, while mdm2 acts as its p53-negative regulator.
- In mdm2/mdm4 mutant zebrafish, p53 tumor-suppressive signaling is activated, showing that Mdm2 normally keeps p53 in check.
- Liver-specific mdm2 expression causes growth retardation and liver fragility, whereas early embryonic mdm2 overexpression did not induce tumors.
- The zebrafish p53–Mdm2 interaction is conserved mainly at the level of functional binding affinity rather than strict sequence identity of the binding motif.
- Alternative splicing and promoter usage can co-regulate tp53 and mdm2 expression in zebrafish.

Q: Which methods are commonly used for gene knockout in zebrafish?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The standard gene knockout method in zebrafish is CRISPR-Cas9 mutagenesis, with invertible gene-trap and zMADM mosaic approaches used for more specialized purposes.

- CRISPR-Cas9 knockout: sgRNA and Cas9 are microinjected into one-cell embryos to generate indels and knockout founder lines [1].
- Conditional knockout: an invertible gene-trap cassette enables tissue- or stage-specific gene disruption after inversion [2].
- Single-cell/mosaic knockout: zMADM (zebrafish mosaic analysis with double markers) allows single-cell gene knockout and dual-lineage tracing [3].

[35/39]  Q26    （2.5 · T7-26）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Is spinal curvature in zebrafish genetically determined?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes — spinal curvature in zebrafish can be genetically determined, but it is not exclusively genetic.

- Zebrafish mutants affecting planar cell polarity, cilia, notochord, or extracellular matrix genes can develop heritable scoliosis/kyphosis-like curvature; for example, ptk7 mutant zebrafish show late-onset spinal curvature.
- Similar curvature can also arise from non-genetic causes, such as vitamin C deficiency, nutritional imbalance, toxins, or poor husbandry.
- Therefore, a curved spine alone should not be assumed to be genetic; heritability, segregation, or identification of a known mutation is needed to confirm genetic determination.

Q: What is the role of asip1 in countershading of body colour?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
asip1 (UniProt · Bgee · Expression Atlas) establishes countershading by creating a dorsoventral gradient of pigment-cell differentiation: higher Asip1 activity ventrally suppresses melanophore differentiation, producing a pale belly, while lower Asip1 dorsally permits melanophores and a dark back.

- Pigment-cell gradient: In zebrafish, Asip1 controls the dorsoventral gradient of pigment-cell differentiation rather than simply total pigmentation, generating the dark dorsal / pale ventral countershading pattern.
- Conserved mechanism: This is consistent with ASIP acting through melanocortin signaling as a secreted regulator/antagonist in fish; asip1 overexpression also alters melanocortin-system outputs.

Q: What literature exists on larval kidney function models in zebrafish?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Larval zebrafish kidney function is modeled mainly in toxicology and imaging-based phenotyping studies, with glomerular filtration rate as a key functional readout.

- Corticosteroid exposure alters kidney development and increases glomerular filtration rate in larval zebrafish, supporting the larval kidney as a functional model [1].
- Organic UV filters have been tested in embryo-larval zebrafish for kidney toxicity alongside thyroid and neurodevelopmental endpoints [2].
- An automated imaging pipeline has been developed for quantitative analysis of the zebrafish larval kidney, enabling higher-throughput functional/phenotypic screening [3].

[39/39]  Q30    （2.5 · T7-30）
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q: Does temperature affect morphological development in zebrafish?
----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, temperature affects zebrafish morphological development. Zebrafish are ectothermic, so developmental rate and morphology are temperature-sensitive.

- Developmental rate: Higher temperatures within the tolerable range accelerate somitogenesis, hatching, and overall growth; lower temperatures slow development. Standard zebrafish husbandry uses ~28.5°C.
- Morphological defects: Elevated or fluctuating temperatures during early development can cause axial curvature, shorter body length, craniofacial abnormalities, and cardiac edema.
- Pigmentation and sex differentiation: Temperature can also influence pigmentation and, in some strains/conditions, sex determination or gonadal differentiation.

Extreme heat stress is teratogenic, while mild changes mainly alter developmental timing and body size.
