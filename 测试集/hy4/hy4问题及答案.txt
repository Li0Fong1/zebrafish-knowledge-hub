- **Pan-cardiomyocyte early, then regionalizes**: Initially uniform across the heart tube; later becomes relatively **higher in atrium** (and outflow tract in some species) than ventricle in zebrafish/chick, while remaining broadly cardiac in mouse. Don’t assume equal chamber intensity at later stages.
- **Isoform switching**: *Myl7* is the **embryonic/atrial-type** regulatory light chain; postnatally it is partially replaced by **MYL2 (ventricular)** in ventricles and persists mainly in atria/conduction system in mammals. Adult “cardiac” signal ≠ embryonic pattern.
- **Transgenic reporter lag/mosaic**: *myl7*:GFP/memCherry lines (especially zebrafish *Tg(myl7:...)*) show **maternal/perdurance effects**, delayed fluorophore maturation, and occasional **ectopic/weak signal in skeletal muscle or pharyngeal mesoderm** at early somite stages—validate with endogenous mRNA/protein.
- **Species-specific timing**: Zebrafish *myl7* precedes heartbeat; mouse *Myl7* coincides with first contractions; human fetal *MYL7* declines after mid-gestation. Avoid direct stage-to-stage extrapolation.
- **Not proliferation- or conduction-specific**: Expression tracks differentiation status, not cell cycle; it labels both working myocytes and early conduction precursors, so it cannot by itself distinguish subtypes.

**Rule of thumb**: use *myl7* as a **differentiation/whole-heart label from heart-tube onward**, but pair with *nkx2.5* (progenitors), *myl2/vmhc* (ventricle), *nr2f1a/atrial* markers, and stage-matched endogenous detection for chamber or maturity claims.

Compare the relationship between tp53 and mdm2 in zebrafish.Answer in English. Be brief.

In zebrafish, as in mammals, **Mdm2 binds Tp53 to inhibit its transcriptional activity and target it for proteasomal degradation**, forming a negative-feedback loop (Tp53 induces *mdm2*; Mdm2 suppresses Tp53).

Key zebrafish-specific notes:
- The interaction is **conserved**, but zebrafish Mdm2 lacks the N-terminal "lid" found in human MDM2, relying more on the canonical hydrophobic pocket.
- **Mdm2 is essential for embryonic viability**: *mdm2* loss causes massive p53-dependent apoptosis, rescued by *tp53* knockdown/knockout.
- Zebrafish also have a **teleost-specific *mdm2* paralogue (*mdm2b*)** from the fish-specific genome duplication; it retains p53 binding but has weaker degradation activity and partially redundant/antagonistic roles.
- Unlike mice, adult *tp53*⁻/⁻ zebrafish are viable but develop tumors late (mostly peripheral nerve sheath tumors), making the Mdm2–Tp53 axis a popular *in vivo* drug-screening target (e.g., Nutlin-3a stabilizes zebrafish Tp53).

**Bottom line:** Same core negative-feedback circuit, with an extra fish paralogue and distinct developmental dependency.

Which methods are commonly used for gene knockout in zebrafish?Answer in English. Be brief.

Common gene-knockout methods in zebrafish:

1. **CRISPR/Cas9** – most widely used; inject Cas9 mRNA/protein + sgRNA into 1-cell embryos to induce indels or deletions.
2. **TALENs** – older, still used for targeted indels; high specificity.
3. **Zinc-finger nucleases (ZFNs)** – largely historical.
4. **Insertional mutagenesis** – retroviral or **Tol2/ Sleeping Beauty transposon** gene-trap lines disrupting genes.
5. **ENU/EMS chemical mutagenesis + TILLING** – random point mutations screened for null alleles.
6. **Morpholinos** – transient *knockdown* (not true knockout; often used for quick validation, with caveats).
7. **CRISPR-based large deletions / CRISPR-Cas12a, base/prime editing** – for precise nulls or conditional approaches (e.g., **Cre/loxP**, **Gal4/UAS**-linked CRISPR).

*Gold standard today: CRISPR/Cas9-generated stable germline null lines.*

What is the role of asip1 in countershading of body colour?Answer in English. Be brief.

**Asip1 (Agouti-signaling protein 1)** establishes the **ventral light colour** in countershading by locally antagonizing melanocortin signalling:

- It is **preferentially expressed in ventral skin**, where it acts as an **inverse agonist/antagonist of Mc1r**, switching melanocytes from **eumelanin (dark)** to **pheomelanin (light/yellow)** production (and/or reducing melanocyte differentiation).
- Dorsal skin shows little/no *asip1*, leaving Mc1r active → dark eumelanin.
- This **dorsoventral gradient of Asip1** thus generates the classic **dark-back / pale-belly** pattern; experimental overexpression ventralises/lightens the dorsum, while knockdown darkens the ventrum (shown in fish such as medaka and zebrafish, and conserved logic in mammals).

In short: **Asip1 is the ventral “lightening” signal that creates countershading.**

What literature exists on larval kidney function models in zebrafish?Answer in English. Be brief.

Literature on **larval zebrafish kidney (pronephros) function models** is concentrated in nephrology, developmental biology, and toxicology. Key themes and representative works:

**Foundational anatomy & physiology**
- Drummond et al. (1998, *Dev Biol*); Drummond & Davidson (2010, *JASN*) – pronephros structure, glomerular filtration barrier, segmental solute transporters.

- Hentschel et al. (2005, *Am J Physiol Renal Physiol*) – FITC-inulin clearance to quantify GFR in larvae.
- Rider et al. (2012, *PLoS One*); Kotb et al. (2014, *J Vis Exp*) – standardized fluorescent dextran/inulin GFR protocols.
- Hanke et al. (2013, *Kidney Int*) – glomerular permeability/size-selectivity modeling.

**Tubular transport & ion/water homeostasis**
- Wingert et al. (2007, *PLoS Genet*) – retinoic acid–defined transporter segments; solute uptake models.
- Abbas et al. (2013, *JASN*) – TRPM6/Mg²⁺ handling; Christou-Savina et al. (2015) – Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase and osmoregulation.
- McDonald et al. (2020, *Front Physiol*) – larval fluid balance and tubular reabsorption modeling.

**Disease / injury models**
- Johnson et al. (2010, *JASN*) – gentamicin-induced AKI and tubular regeneration.
- Cianciolo Cosentino et al. (2013, *Development*) – podocyte injury / proteinuria model.
- Morales & Wingert (2017, *Genesis*); McCampbell & Wingert (2014, *Wiley Interdiscip Rev Dev Biol*) – reviews of polycystic kidney & ciliopathy models.

**Computational / quantitative models**
- Less common than empirical assays; mostly **pharmacokinetic (PBPK-style) clearance models** and **agent-based ciliary-flow simulations** (e.g., Roxo-Rosa et al., 2015, *Biophys J*; Ferreira et al., 2019, *Front Physiol*). No widely adopted whole-kidney systems model exists for larvae yet.

- Outtandy et al. (2019, *Clin Kidney J*); Gehrig et al. (2018, *Kidney Int Rep*) – larval GFR/AKI assay comparisons.

**Gap**: Most literature is *experimental assay–based*; explicit mathematical/Systems Biology models of larval kidney function remain sparse (clearance curves + flow CFD dominate).

Does temperature affect morphological development in zebrafish?Answer in English. Be brief.

Yes. Temperature significantly affects zebrafish morphological development: warmer water (within the viable range, ~25–33 °C) accelerates somitogenesis, hatching, and organ formation, while cooler water (~22–25 °C) delays them. Extreme temperatures cause deformities (e.g., spinal curvature, craniofacial defects, edema) and reduced survival. The standard lab temperature is 28.5 °C.
